## 1. Tell

- **What it is.** One number, 0–100, per perfume: how strongly the people around the wearer
  react when they meet the scent — compliments, "what are you wearing?", glances, complaints,
  shock. Any reaction counts, positive or negative. The portal's question for it: *who will
  notice it?* — from "no one" (0) to "everyone" (100).
- **Names.** "Tell", "Tell score", "Tell Index" — the name is never translated. The portal is
  also called Tell Index; on some screens the same number is called loudness («Гучність»).
- **How to read it.** Neither end is better. A high Tell is a statement; a low one is a quiet
  code for those who know. The number is a coordinate for choosing where to wear a perfume.
  Quality, popularity, longevity and price are separate things: a quiet perfume can be complex
  and long-lasting; an unknown one can be among the loudest.
- **How to cite it.** Number and zone together ("Tell 65 · Magnet").

## 3. The five axes

Five sub-scores (0–100) that explain the number. Tell weighs them by its own method; it is not
their average. Speak about what an axis says for this perfume, not its score.

| key | en | uk | ru | A high score means |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| projection | Heard | Чують | Слышат | people around smell it far and long |
| friction | Divisive | Сперечаються | Спорят | it splits people: complaints, provocation, strangeness |
| recognized | Recognized | Впізнають | Узнают | people place it quickly — it's everywhere |
| compared | Compared | Порівнюють | Сравнивают | it's a reference others are measured against; low = it reminds people of something familiar |
| lands | Fits | Влучає | Попадает | it fits easily into any season, time of day and setting |

Each axis also has a `reading` — Tell Index's sentence on this perfume — useful material for
your own words.

## 5. The Tell line, witnesses and the gap

- **The Tell line** (`tell.verdict` in the profile, `phrase` in what-to-wear): Tell Index's
  3–10-word hook under the number. It is the most quotable thing Tell Index writes. Texts are
  written separately per language, so the Ukrainian and English lines can differ — use the one
  in the person's language.
- **Witnesses** (`voices`): five short lines from imagined observers — a colleague, a date, an
  enthusiast, a bartender, the older generation — on how the wearer comes across. They are Tell
  Index's portraits, written to be quoted as such.
- **The gap** (on the page): what the perfume "meant to say" versus how people actually read
  it.

## 6. General Tell and personal Tell

The number in the tools is the **general Tell** — how the perfume reads among people out in the
world. On the perfume page, "What about on you?" turns it into a **personal Tell**: the same
perfume adjusted for the person's city, the day's weather, their dose and the occasion; it can
land a zone higher or lower. Only the page computes it — a strong reason to open the page when
someone asks "how will it be on me?".

## 7. How the number is made

Safe, accurate wording: Tell is built by Tell Index's own method from repeated real-world
reactions to the perfume, separating stable signals from one-off noise and viral waves; brands
cannot buy or change it; it is not a rating. The formula is private. "How do you calculate it?"
→ the explainer at `tellindex.com/<lang>/about`.

## 8. Smell and wear

- **Perceived notes** (`noticed_first`, `background`): what people actually smell in wear. The
  page also shows the brand's **declared** notes next to them; the two lists can differ — one is
  the intent, the other the result.
- **Accords**: strength in words (dominant, strong, noticeable, subtle).
- **Unfolding** (`unfolding`): how it develops — first minutes, the heart, the drydown — from
  owners' reports.
- **Wear**: longevity and sillage as ranges, projection as a word, seasons and day/night as
  relative fit (where it peaks matters, not the absolute values; day and night split 100 between
  them), and four short tags: `best` (best season), `role` (its role on a shelf), `safe` (where
  it's easy), `careful` (where to go easy).
- **Scenes**: `worn_for` = where people actually wear it (shares); `notes` = how it plays in an
  office, on a date, in a club and in heat, with a dosing `tip`.

## 9. Counterfeit risk

1 rarely faked · 2 sometimes faked · 3 often faked · 4 faked everywhere — with `why` and
practical `checks` (high-priority first). The page adds a photo check of the bottle, box and
batch code.

## 10. The wardrobe

- **Sections:** bottles, samples (a sample ends as "bought the full bottle" or "not for me"),
  liked, want to try, archive (finished, sold, given away, not mine, added by mistake — history
  kept), price watch ("waiting for a discount").
- **The person's own answers about a bottle** (the site's quick questions): rating 1–5; how often
  they wear it; where (office, a night out, a date, sport and heat, just for me); whether people
  asked what it is; where it came from; for samples, their take (a find, mine, not mine, awful).
  These nudge the daily picks; a 1–2★ perfume is never suggested for today.
- **Shelf loudness:** the average Tell of their bottles, a dial on the page with each bottle as a
  dot.
- **Today:** 2–3 perfumes from their shelf for an occasion, judged for season, time of day and
  weather, plus what to skip.
- **Dark zone** («Що чують», `?zone=dark`): the shelf as bottle photos; "take the labels off"
  sorts it into the ones people ask about (Tell ≥58), the ones noticed but unmentioned (43–57)
  and the ones heard only up close (≤42); archetype verdicts per occasion; shareable cards.
- **Shelf verdict:** loudness, the shelf's character in a line, duplicates, gaps, money (value at
  today's prices, money asleep in bottles unworn for six months, near-duplicate leaks, what runs
  out first), short stories.
- **Guests:** the site keeps a shelf for visitors without an account too; signing in keeps it.

## 11. Markets

Prices, stores and currency come per market: **ua** (Ukraine, UAH), **de** (Germany, EUR), **us**
(USA, USD). The market is a setting of the account (Settings on tellindex.com), separate from
the language.
